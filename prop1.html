01

Introduction & project overview

Purpose, scope, stakeholders and the platform's organizing principles.

1.1 Purpose

This specification defines a proposed digital platform for Shakti Sabha, intended to present women's empowerment initiatives, community programmes, educational resources and public discussion in one coherent experience. It is a planning baseline, not proof that every listed service or impact metric already exists.

1.2 Scope

Included in the product vision
  • Bilingual interface with accessible language switching.
  • Editorial pages, team profiles, stories and impact reporting.
  • Social work programme pages and carefully moderated case studies.
  • Events, volunteer interest forms and programme registrations.
  • Courses, learning resources and research publications.
  • Optional donations, paid registrations and course payments.
  • Role-based administration, analytics and content review.
Requires separate decisions
  • Live counselling or emergency-response service.
  • Storage of survivor identities or detailed case files.
  • Mobile applications, offline access or messaging integrations.
  • International donations, tax receipts or recurring billing.
  • Certificates with formal accreditation or external recognition.
  • Any impact statistics not supported by auditable records.

1.3 Core pillars

Revolution

Mission Mardini

Self-defence awareness, confidence building, rights education and practical community workshops.

Peace & healing

Shakti Bhava

Supportive resources, stigma-aware education and signposting to qualified services. The site must not imply clinical care unless staffed and governed for it.

Intellectual discourse

Shakti Varta

Interviews, seminars, podcasts and respectful dialogue about equality, responsibility and social change.

Impact metrics: figures such as “12 years” or “400+ schools” appeared in the supplied draft. Publish them only after the organization verifies the reporting period, definitions and source records.

  • Use responsive image sources and lazy-load below-the-fold images.
  • Provide descriptive alt text for informative images; empty alt for decorative images.
  • Compress assets based on visual quality and real-device testing.

Founder and team profiles

  • Timeline and achievements with evidence or review.
  • Team, partner and volunteer roles.

06

UI/UX, motion & interactions

A calm, accessible interface with purposeful animation rather than decoration that competes with content.

Navigation and layout

  • Responsive navigation with clear labels and active states.
  • Consistent page hierarchy, spacing and reusable cards.
  • Search and filters for articles, events and courses where useful.
  • Visible language switcher that preserves the current page where translations exist.
  • Breadcrumbs on deep content pages and clear empty states.

Motion and feedback

  • Subtle hover, focus and pressed states.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Use animation to explain state changes, not to block reading.
  • Forms provide field-level errors and a clear success confirmation.
  • Do not rely on hover for essential information on touch screens.

Accessibility and quality targets

AreaRequirementVerification
AccessibilityTarget WCAG 2.2 AA; semantic headings, keyboard access, visible focus, labels and contrast.Automated scan plus keyboard and assistive-technology testing.

08

Case study blog

Ethical storytelling that preserves dignity and avoids exposing people to additional risk.

Publishing features

  • Category, date, author, summary and language version.
  • Review workflow and correction / takedown path.
  • Image captions, alt text and source attribution.
  • Search-friendly URLs and social sharing metadata.
  • Related programme links and moderated comments only if justified.

Safeguarding rules

  • Obtain informed, specific consent for identifiable stories and images.
  • Offer anonymization and remove unnecessary identifying details.
  • Do not publish addresses, live locations or details that can identify a survivor indirectly.
  • Explain where and how a story may be distributed.
  • Maintain a documented correction, withdrawal and escalation process.
Data minimization: the public case-study blog should contain approved, redacted narratives—not raw intake forms, counselling notes, medical records or legal documents.

Events management

A manageable path from event discovery to attendance and follow-up.

Visitor capabilities

  • Browse upcoming and past events.
  • View date, time zone, venue or online format, accessibility notes and capacity.
  • Register and receive confirmation where contact details are supplied.
  • See cancellation, refund and attendance terms before paying.
  • Join a waitlist if enabled and capacity is reached.

Organizer capabilities

  • Create and edit event listings with approval permissions.
  • Set capacity, registration window and fee.
  • Export only necessary attendee data to authorized organizers.
  • Record check-in and send operational updates.
  • Close registration and retain/delete records under a defined policy.

Calendar integrations, QR check-in, automated messaging and waitlists are optional features that affect scope and cost.

Payments & financial sustainability

Use a payment provider rather than handling card data directly.

Possible revenue / support paths

  • Donations with a clear purpose and acknowledgement.
  • Paid course enrolment and event tickets.
  • Merchandise only if stock, fulfilment and returns are managed.
  • Institutional partnerships and sponsored programmes.
  • Optional recurring contributions if the provider and compliance setup support them.

Checkout requirements

  • Show total price, currency, applicable taxes and cancellation terms.
  • Use hosted checkout or provider-approved secure components.
  • Verify payment status server-side; never trust a browser success page alone.
  • Process signed webhooks idempotently and reconcile failed / duplicate notifications.
  • Issue receipts only when the organization can support accurate records.
India-specific verification: before accepting donations, confirm the organization's legal status, bank account ownership, tax treatment and any applicable FCRA requirements for foreign contributions. Do not promise tax deductibility or foreign-donation eligibility without qualified review.

Payment gateway fees, settlement times, tax handling and supported payment methods depend on the selected provider and contract; obtain current written pricing before budgeting.

Security, privacy & anonymity

Privacy-by-design controls for a platform whose content may concern vulnerable people.

Baseline controls

  • HTTPS in transit and encryption at rest provided by configured services.
  • Role-based access, least privilege and multi-factor authentication for administrators.
  • Secure session and password handling through a maintained identity provider.
  • Input validation, output encoding, CSRF protections where applicable, and rate limiting.
  • Secrets stored in a managed secret store, not committed to source control.
  • Dependency updates, audit logging, backups and a tested restore process.

Sensitive-data controls

  • Collect only information necessary for a stated purpose.
  • Keep survivor support details out of analytics, public CMS and routine logs.
  • Separate public stories from private case-management records.
  • Define access, retention, deletion and incident-response procedures.
  • Use private storage and short-lived signed links where appropriate.
  • Offer a discreet exit/navigation option only if its limitations are explained; browser history cannot be reliably erased by a website.

Important clarification: “zero-knowledge”

The supplied draft mentions zero-knowledge encryption. That phrase is not a feature that can be guaranteed merely by using a cloud provider. If the platform must be unable to read sensitive records, the architecture needs a threat model, client-side encryption/key management design, recovery strategy and usability review. Otherwise, describe the actual controls precisely as encryption in transit, encryption at rest and restricted access—not zero-knowledge encryption.

Technology stack & architecture

A maintainable starting architecture; final choices depend on team skills, traffic and operating budget.

Proposed stack

  • Web: Next.js with TypeScript and a responsive component system.
  • Styling: Tailwind CSS or a documented CSS design system.
  • Content: Headless CMS or a carefully scoped custom admin.
  • Data: Managed PostgreSQL for structured records where appropriate.
  • Media: Private/public object storage with access policies.
  • Hosting: Managed platform or AWS architecture selected after cost and operational review.
  • Payments: Provider-hosted checkout and server-side webhook verification.

Delivery timeline & milestones

A phased plan designed to validate core needs before investing in complex modules.

20-Day Showable Website
⚡ What we deliver in 20 days: A showable website — a polished, client-presentable version of the platform with core modules (homepage, brand identity, profiles, social work, case studies, events, research section, and basic CMS). This is not the complete website with all bells and whistles. The full production-ready platform — including complete LMS, payment integration, advanced security hardening, bilingual content review, and all optional modules — will follow in subsequent phases.

What “showable” means: a live, navigable website that demonstrates the design, brand, content structure, and key user journeys. It is not the final production launch with every feature. Full payment gateway integration, complete LMS, advanced security hardening, full bilingual content, and all optional modules are delivered in later phases. This 20-day plan gives you something real to show to stakeholders, donors, and partners — fast.

⚠️ Important: The 20-day timeline assumes: (1) content (text, images) is provided or approved within the first 3 days; (2) feedback rounds are completed within 24 hours; (3) no major scope changes after Day 5; (4) advance payment is confirmed before Day 1. Any delay in content or approvals will shift the timeline.

What is NOT included in the 20-day showable website (next phases)

  • Full payment gateway integration (Razorpay/Stripe) — Phase 2
  • Complete LMS with video streaming, quizzes, certificates — Phase 2
  • Advanced security hardening & penetration testing — Phase 2
  • Full bilingual content (Hindi + English) — Phase 2
  • Complete Sahyog workflow with application tracking — Phase 3
  • Merchandise store & order management — Phase 3
  • 12-year media archive ingestion — Phase 3
  • User accounts & personalised dashboards — Phase 3
  • Advanced analytics & reporting — Phase 3
  • Mobile app (PWA is included in showable) — Future
  • Third-party integrations (calendar, email marketing) — Phase 2
  • SEO optimization & full content audit — Phase 2

Launch acceptance checklist (for showable website)

  • All core pages work on mobile and desktop.
  • Brand identity, logo, and 4-color palette are applied consistently.
  • Navigation and main user journeys are demonstrable.
  • Sample content is in place for each section.
  • Basic admin/CMS access is demonstrated to the client.
  • Site is deployed to a shareable staging URL.
  • No broken links or missing pages in the main nav.
  • Client has received a walkthrough of the site.
  • Documentation for next phases is provided.
  • Named owner accepts post-handover support.
